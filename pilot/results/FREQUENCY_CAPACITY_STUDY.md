# Frequency-and-capacity study (SIREN coordinate network) — measured results

Generated by `pilot/summarize_frequency_study.py` from `pilot/results/frequency_capacity_runs.csv`, `frequency_capacity_curves.jsonl`, `frequency_capacity_selection.json` and `frequency_capacity_env.json`. Every number below is measured; nothing is estimated or typed by hand.

## What this experiment is (and is not)

- **Supervised regression on analytical labels**: MSE between network output and h(x,y,t)=sin(k(x−0.5t)) at sampled points. **Not physics-informed**: no PDE residual is in the loss.
- Train/val/test are independent uniform samples from the **same domain** (x,y∈[0,2π], t∈[0,2]). Test error measures **in-domain interpolation**, not temporal or out-of-domain generalisation.
- One fixed split for every run (data seed 20261023): 2000 train / 500 val / 500 test. Run seeds vary only initialisation (full-batch Adam has no other randomness).
- All selection (w0_first, w0_hidden, lr, epoch budget, tanh lr, best checkpoint) used **validation MSE only**, on selection seeds 100–102. Test MSE was computed only for the final phases (seeds 0–4) and for the ablation, and never used to choose anything.
- 'y-resample Δ²' = **mean squared prediction change under y resampling** (test points with y redrawn). It measures unwanted dependence on y; it is not a variance and is not used as evidence of memorisation. Memorisation is discussed only via the val/train MSE ratio at the best checkpoint.
- rel L2² = ‖pred−target‖²/‖target‖² = test squared error as a fraction of the zero-predictor's squared error.
- Threshold success = runs whose best val MSE ≤ 1e-4 / total runs, kept in its own column, separate from test error. Runs that did not get there are reported as 'threshold not reached'.
- **Error summaries (val, train, test MSE, rel L2, Δ²) are median [min, max] over ALL seeds of a configuration, successful and failed alike**, not only successful runs.
- Cost evidence: everything ran on this CPU-only box, so **no cloud charges** were incurred; the cost is CPU wall time (reported below and per run). This is not a claim that the work cost nothing.

## Findings (interpretation of the measured tables below)

1. **Frequency at fixed architecture (Study A, SIREN 32×2, 1217 params, w0 3.0/10.0, lr 0.003, 10000 epochs).** k=1: success 5/5, test rel L2 1.22e-03 [1.11e-03, 1.60e-03]; k=2: success 5/5, test rel L2 1.56e-03 [1.42e-03, 2.47e-03]; k=4: success 5/5, test rel L2 1.52e-03 [9.79e-04, 2.01e-03]; k=8: success 0/5, test rel L2 1.00e+00 [8.73e-01, 1.00e+00]. k=1–4 all reach val MSE ≤ 1e-4; k=8 fails every seed and its test rel L2 ≈ 1 means its best-val checkpoint is no better than predicting zero.
2. **Capacity did not rescue k=8 for the 3-input models.** No SIREN or tanh width/depth configuration reached the threshold at k=8 (SIREN width 16/64/128: 0/5, 0/5, 0/5; depth 1/3/4: 0/5, 0/5, 0/5). Wider/deeper SIRENs drive k=8 train MSE down but val stays high (classified 'poor generalisation' below); the smallest models stall. At k≤4, larger SIRENs (width 64/128, depth 3/4) had **higher** median test rel L2 than 32×2 under this fixed lr/budget, and depth 3/4 lost successes at k=4 (4/5, 3/5); the smaller width-16 and depth-1 SIRENs matched or beat 32×2 at k=1–2 (depth 1 then dropped to 1/5 at k=4).
3. **(x,t)-only diagnostic:** the same SIREN with y removed reaches the threshold at k=8 in 5/5 seeds (test rel L2 9.94e-03 [4.88e-03, 1.14e-02]) versus 0/5 with (x,y,t). Removing y also changes input dimension, first-layer init bound and parameter count (1185 vs 1217), so this implicates the 3-input setup but does not isolate the cause.
4. **Ablation (k=1, original w0 30/30, lr 1e-3):** success off/1000 0/5, off/5000 0/5, on/1000 0/5, on/5000 5/5. Under these tested settings the benefit is the **combination** of normalisation and the longer budget; neither alone was sufficient.
5. **w0=30 hypothesis:** refuted for this task and grid. w0 30/30 scored -2.84 (mean log10 val MSE) vs the selected 3.0/10.0 at -4.19; separating first-layer and hidden frequency mattered (w0_hidden=1 was poor for every w0_first).
6. **SIREN vs matched-capacity tanh:** both succeed at k=1–2 at every matched size tested; at k=4 SIREN 32×2 got 5/5 vs tanh 32×2 3/5; at k=8 both fail all seeds (SIREN by poor generalisation, tanh 32×2 by partial fit).
7. **Latency:** with the identical procedure, the analytical formula was faster than every network in every run (network/formula median ratio 1.3× to 15.9×). No speed advantage for the surrogate is supported.
8. **Cost:** 572 training runs, summed training wall time 76.7 min across workers (study wall clock 667.2 s, from the console log), CPU only, **no cloud charges**.

### Caveats

- One fixed data split (seed 20261023); 5 init seeds per finalist config. Results could shift with another split.
- Selected lr (0.003) and budget (10000) are at the **edge** of their grids, so better values may lie outside them. They were selected separately (one factor at a time, averaged over k) and their combination was not validated before the final phase; at k=8 the lr 1e-3 / 10000-epoch sweep reached the threshold in 1 of 3 selection seeds, while the combined lr 3e-3 / 10000-epoch finalist reached it in 0/5. A per-k selection might change the k=8 picture.
- The tanh baseline got its own lr selection (3 values) but no other tuning; SIREN got a 12-cell w0 grid plus lr and budget sweeps, so the comparison is not equal-effort.
- Training seconds were measured with 7 concurrent workers; use them for relative comparisons only. Latency is 1-thread, batch 500, on this VM only.
- Supervised regression on analytical labels, same-domain interpolation only; nothing here tests physics-informed training, extrapolation in t, or commercial value.
